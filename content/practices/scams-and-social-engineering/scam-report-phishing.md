## Why this matters

Somebody in your organization will eventually click a bad link. What matters is how fast you find out. When people report quickly, even with "I clicked it, sorry", a breach becomes a near miss. Blame makes people hide it.

## Do it

1. **One button or one address:** turn on the "Report phishing" button in Outlook or Gmail, or create `phish@yourdomain` or a chat channel.
2. **Thank every report**, even false alarms. "I clicked" is a report, not a confession, and nobody gets in trouble for reporting.
3. **Act:** if one person got it, others did too. Search and remove it from other inboxes (Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace admins can) and block the sender or domain.
4. **Share examples** monthly: "here's what came in, here's the tell".

> [!success] How do you know it worked?
> Reports come in regularly (zero usually means people don't know how, not that there's no phishing), and you can show how fast you acted on the last one.

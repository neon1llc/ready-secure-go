## Why this matters

What you share adds up: a birthday post, a check-in at your kid's school, a photo of a new car in front of your house, answers to "first pet's name?" quizzes. Together they make a profile that eases scams, account recovery attacks, and stalking. Much of it is shared by default settings, not by choice.

> [!question] Ask yourself
> - Which apps see your location *all the time*? Do they need to?

## Do it

1. **Phone permissions:** on iPhone, Settings > Privacy & Security (also run **Safety Check**). On Android, Settings > Security & privacy > Privacy dashboard. Turn off location, microphone, contacts, and photos access for apps that don't need them. Prefer "While Using" over "Always".
2. **Google:** myaccount.google.com/privacycheckup. Review Web & App Activity, Location History/Timeline, and ad personalization.
3. **Apple:** review Significant Locations and analytics sharing.
4. **Social media:** make accounts private or friends-only, limit who can find you by phone number or email, hide your friends list, and turn off photo location tags.
5. **Remove** old posts that reveal addresses, schedules, or security-question answers.
6. **Security questions:** don't answer truthfully. Make up answers and store them in your password manager.
7. **Kids' accounts:** use the platform's teen settings and family pairing, and go through them *with* your kid. See [Have the family tech talk, and keep having it](../people-and-culture/ppl-family-tech-talk.md).

> [!success] How do you know it worked?
> A logged-out search of your name shows only what you're comfortable with, and no app has "Always" location without a good reason.

> [!danger] Attacker's-eye view
> Attackers research targets on public sites and social media (MITRE ATT&CK T1593, Search Open Websites/Domains, and T1589, Gather Victim Identity Information).

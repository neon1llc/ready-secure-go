## Why this matters

Your Social Security number has very likely leaked in a breach already. With it, a criminal can open credit cards, loans, or phone accounts in your name. A **credit freeze** blocks lenders from seeing your credit report, so those applications fail. It's free, doesn't hurt your credit score, and lifts in minutes when *you* need credit. Kids are prime targets because nobody checks a child's credit until they're 18.

## Do it

1. Create accounts and freeze at **all three** bureaus:
   - Equifax: equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services
   - Experian: experian.com/freeze
   - TransUnion: transunion.com/credit-freeze
2. Save each login or PIN in your password manager. You need it to lift the freeze.
3. **Kids under 16:** parents can request a freeze. Each bureau has a page for minors, usually asking for copies of the birth certificate and Social Security card.
4. **Older relatives** you care for: help them freeze too.
5. Also consider a freeze with **ChexSystems** (bank accounts) and **NCTUE** (utilities and phone), plus an **IRS Identity Protection PIN** to stop tax-refund fraud.
6. When you apply for credit, **temporarily lift** the freeze at the bureau the lender uses, then refreeze.
7. **Fraud alert:** if you can't freeze right now, a free fraud alert asks lenders to verify your identity. Contact one bureau and it tells the other two. An initial alert lasts one year. With an identity theft report from IdentityTheft.gov, an extended alert lasts seven years. A freeze is stronger, and it does not cover accounts you already have, so keep watching those statements.

> [!success] How do you know it worked?
> All three bureau sites show "frozen", and the logins are saved.

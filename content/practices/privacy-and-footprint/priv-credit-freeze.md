## Why this matters

Your Social Security number has very likely leaked in at least one breach already. With it, a criminal can open credit cards, loans or phone accounts in your name. A **credit freeze** blocks lenders from seeing your credit report, so those applications fail. It's free, it doesn't hurt your credit score, and you can lift it in minutes when *you* need credit.

Kids are prime targets because nobody checks a child's credit until they're 18. By then the damage can be years old.

## Ask yourself

- If someone opened a credit card in your name today, how would you find out?
- Have you ever checked whether your child has a credit report? (They shouldn't.)

## Do it

1. Create accounts and freeze at **all three** bureaus:
   - Equifax: equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services
   - Experian: experian.com/freeze
   - TransUnion: transunion.com/credit-freeze
2. Save each login or PIN in your password manager. You'll need it to lift the freeze.
3. **Kids under 16:** parents can request a freeze for them. Each bureau has a page for minors, usually requiring a birth certificate and Social Security card copies.
4. **Older relatives** you care for: help them freeze too. It's one of the best protections against elder financial exploitation.
5. Also consider: freezing with **ChexSystems** (bank accounts) and **NCTUE** (utilities and phone), plus an **IRS Identity Protection PIN** to stop tax-refund fraud.
6. When you apply for credit, **temporarily lift** the freeze at the bureau the lender uses, then it refreezes.

## How do you know it worked?

All three bureau sites show your status as "frozen", and the logins are saved.

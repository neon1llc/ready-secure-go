## Why this matters

Annual training done to tick a box is forgotten by lunch. What works is short sessions, real examples from *your* environment, and practice. People who understand *why* a scam works catch the next one even when it looks different.

## Do it

1. **Monthly, 10 to 15 minutes:** one topic, one real example, one action. Rotate through scams, passwords and MFA, devices, physical security, privacy, and reporting.
2. **Use your own examples:** the phishing emails your team actually got, with names removed.
3. **Practice:** simulated phishing helps *if* it teaches, not shames. Never punish clicks. Celebrate reports.
4. **Onboarding:** a 15-minute welcome on day one covering how to report, the payment-verification rule ([Never change payment details on the strength of an email](../scams-and-social-engineering/scam-payment-change-verification.md)), MFA setup, and where the policies live.
5. **Leaders go first.** When the boss says "I almost fell for this one," reporting becomes normal.
6. **Track simply:** who attended and how fast reports come in. SOC 2 auditors ask for training records.

> [!success] How do you know it worked?
> Phish reports go up and time-to-report goes down.

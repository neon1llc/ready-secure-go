title: "Playbook: I think I got scammed"
description: "Stop more money from leaving, try to get back what already left, and protect your identity, without shame."
tags: [playbook]
---

First: **this happens to smart, careful people every day.** Scammers are professionals. Feeling embarrassed is normal, and it's exactly what they count on to keep you quiet. Acting fast matters more than anything else.

## Right now

1. **Stop contact.** Hang up, don't reply, and don't send "one more" payment, even if they threaten you or promise a refund. "Recovery" offers from strangers are a second scam.
2. **Call your bank or card company** using the number on your card or in their official app. Say: *"I've been the victim of fraud. I need to stop or recall a payment."* Ask about:
   - **Card payments:** dispute the charge.
   - **Wire transfers:** ask for a wire recall *immediately*. Hours count.
   - **Zelle or bank transfers:** report it as fraud and ask about the bank's process.
   - **Gift cards:** call the card's company (the number is on the card or its website) and report it. Keep the cards and receipts.
   - **Crypto:** report to the exchange you used. Recovery is hard, but report anyway.
3. **If you gave remote access to your computer** ("tech support" scams): disconnect it from the internet, and have it checked or reset before using it for banking.
4. **If you shared passwords or codes:** change those passwords now and turn on two-step login ([Playbook: my email or social media account was hacked](hacked-email.md)).

## Today

- **If you shared your Social Security number or ID:** freeze your credit at all three bureaus ([Freeze your credit (and your kids' credit)](../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-credit-freeze.md)) and go to **IdentityTheft.gov** for a recovery plan.
- **Report it:**
  - **ReportFraud.ftc.gov** (all fraud)
  - **ic3.gov** (internet-related crime, especially if money moved by wire or crypto)
  - Older adults or their caregivers: **National Elder Fraud Hotline, 1-833-FRAUD-11 (1-833-372-8311)**
  - Local police, if your bank or insurer needs a report number
- **Keep everything:** messages, phone numbers, receipts, transaction IDs, screenshots.

## This week

- Watch your bank and card statements closely.
- Tell family and friends, so the same scammers can't reach them. Talking about it is how scams lose their power.
- Expect follow-up scams: people who've paid once get targeted again, including fake "refund" and "fraud recovery" calls.

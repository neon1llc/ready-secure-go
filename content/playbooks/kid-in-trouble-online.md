If your child came to you, **thank them.** That was brave, and it's the most important step. Stay calm in front of them, even if you're not calm inside. They need to know they're not in trouble and you'll handle it together.

**If your child is in immediate danger or talking about hurting themselves, call 911.** For a mental health crisis, call or text **988**, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

## If someone is threatening to share images (sextortion)

1. **Don't pay and don't send anything more.** Paying almost never ends it. It usually leads to more demands.
2. **Stop responding,** but **don't delete** the account or messages yet. Take screenshots of the profile, usernames, messages and any payment requests.
3. **Report:**
   - **NCMEC CyberTipline:** report.cybertip.org or 1-800-843-5678
   - **FBI:** tips.fbi.gov or 1-800-CALL-FBI
4. **Get images taken down:** use **Take It Down** (takeitdown.ncmec.org) from NCMEC. It helps remove or block explicit images of minors on participating platforms *without* anyone having to send the image itself.
5. **Block and report** the account on the platform after you've saved evidence.

## If it's bullying, harassment or a creepy contact

1. **Save evidence** (screenshots with dates and usernames).
2. **Block and report** on the platform.
3. **Involve the school** if classmates are involved. Most schools have bullying policies that cover online behavior.
4. **Contact police** if there are threats of violence, stalking, or an adult seeking contact with your child.

## Afterwards

- Lock down privacy settings together ([Do a privacy checkup on your big accounts and phone](../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-account-privacy-review.md)).
- Keep checking in, gently, for weeks. Kids often feel ashamed long after the crisis passes.
- Consider talking to a counselor. This is a lot to carry.

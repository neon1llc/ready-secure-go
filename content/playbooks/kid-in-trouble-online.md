title: "Playbook: my child is in trouble online"
description: "Sextortion, threats, harassment, or a scary contact: what to do first, what not to do, and where to get help."
tags: [playbook]
---

If your child came to you, **thank them.** Stay calm. They need to know they're not in trouble and you'll handle it together.

**If your child is in immediate danger or talking about hurting themselves, call 911.** For a mental health crisis, call or text **988**, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

## If someone is threatening to share images (sextortion)

1. **Don't pay and don't send anything more.** Paying almost never ends it and usually brings more demands.
2. **Stop responding,** but **don't delete** the account or messages yet. Screenshot the profile, usernames, messages, and payment requests.
3. **Report:**
   - **NCMEC CyberTipline:** report.cybertip.org or 1-800-843-5678
   - **FBI:** tips.fbi.gov or 1-800-CALL-FBI
4. **Get images taken down** with NCMEC's **Take It Down** (takeitdown.ncmec.org). It removes or blocks explicit images of minors on participating platforms *without* anyone sending the image itself.
5. **Block and report** the account on the platform once evidence is saved.

## If it's bullying, harassment, or a creepy contact

1. **Save evidence** (screenshots with dates and usernames), then **block and report** on the platform.
2. **Involve the school** if classmates are involved.
3. **Contact police** for threats of violence, stalking, or an adult seeking contact with your child.

## Afterwards

Lock down privacy settings together ([Do a privacy checkup on your big accounts and phone](../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-account-privacy-review.md)). Keep checking in gently for weeks, since kids often feel ashamed long after. Consider a counselor.

Breach notices are common, and most don't lead to harm *if* you act on what leaked. Don't panic, but don't ignore it.

## First: is the notice real?

Scammers send fake breach notices to steal information. **Don't click links or call numbers in the notice.** Go to the company's website yourself, or search for news of the breach, and check that it matches.

## Then match your action to what leaked

| What leaked | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Password** | Change it, and anywhere you reused it ([Let a password manager remember for you](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-password-manager.md)). |
| **Email address or phone** | Expect more targeted phishing. Keep two-step login on ([Turn on two-step login, starting with your email](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-two-step-login.md)). |
| **Social Security number** | Freeze credit at all three bureaus ([Freeze your credit (and your kids' credit)](../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-credit-freeze.md)). Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN. |
| **Card number** | Ask your bank for a new card. Watch statements. |
| **Bank account number** | Tell your bank and ask about extra protection. |
| **Medical or insurance info** | Review your insurer's statements ("explanation of benefits") for services you didn't get. |
| **Driver's license or passport** | Watch for identity misuse. Report to IdentityTheft.gov if it happens. |
| **Security question answers** | Change them, and use made-up answers stored in your password manager. |

## Take what they offer

Free credit monitoring is worth accepting. Sign up directly through the company's official site.

## If you're the organization that was breached

- Call your lawyer and cyber insurer first. Notification rules depend on the data, the state and your contracts.
- The FTC's *Data Breach Response: A Guide for Business* is a solid free checklist.
- Be honest, clear and quick with the people affected. Tell them exactly what leaked and what to do, ideally linking to a table like the one above.

**Signs:** password suddenly wrong, friends getting strange messages from you, "new sign-in" alerts you don't recognize, sent mail you didn't write, or forwarding rules you didn't make.

## Right now (first 30 minutes)

1. **Use a device you trust**, not one you think might have malware.
2. **Try to log in and change the password.** If you can't, use the official recovery flow (accounts.google.com/signin/recovery for Google, account.live.com/acsr for Microsoft, iforgot.apple.com, or the platform's "hacked account" page, such as facebook.com/hacked or instagram.com/hacked). Never use "recovery services" you find in ads or that contact you.
3. **Sign out every other session** (security settings, "sign out of all devices").
4. **Turn on two-step login** if it wasn't on, or reset it if the attacker changed it. See [Turn on two-step login, starting with your email](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-two-step-login.md).

## Close the back doors (same day)

Attackers often set things up to keep access even after you change your password:

- **Recovery email and phone:** remove any you don't recognize ([Make sure you can get back into your own accounts](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-recovery-check.md)).
- **Forwarding rules and filters:** in Gmail check Settings > Forwarding and Filters. In Outlook check Rules and Forwarding. Delete anything you didn't create, especially rules that forward or delete messages.
- **Connected apps:** remove apps and devices you don't recognize.
- **Passkeys and security keys:** remove any you didn't add.
- **Email signature and auto-reply:** check for changed payment details.

## Contain the damage

- **Change passwords on every account that resets through this email**, starting with banking, then anything with the same or similar password ([Let a password manager remember for you](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-password-manager.md)).
- **Warn your contacts** (from a clean account or by phone): "My account was hacked. Ignore any request for money or links from me between [dates]."
- **Business email:** tell IT or your provider immediately. Search for invoices or payment-change emails sent from the account ([Never change payment details on the strength of an email](../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-payment-change-verification.md)), check whether the attacker accessed shared files, and consider whether customers must be notified. Call your cyber insurer.
- **If money was involved:** see [Playbook: I think I got scammed](scam-victim.md).

## Afterwards

- Run a malware scan on the devices you use for that account, and update them.
- Report: **ReportFraud.ftc.gov**, or **IdentityTheft.gov** if your identity was misused.
- Write down what happened and when. It helps with banks, insurers and any police report.

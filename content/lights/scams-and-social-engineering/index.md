---
title: Scams & Social Engineering
description: "Phishing, fraud, impersonation, voice clones and payment scams: attacks on attention, not technology."
---

The most effective attacks don't break technology. They borrow your trust and rush your judgment. That's good news, because the defense is a habit anyone can learn: notice the pressure, pause, and verify through a channel you already trust.

## Ask yourself

- What's the last message that made you feel you had to act *right now*? Who benefited from that rush?
- How would you know a call from your bank, your boss or your grandkid was real?
- Who in your organization can send money, and what would stop a fake request?

## Practices

<!-- cards:start -->
### 1 · Spark

- [Agree on a family code word for emergencies](../../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-family-code-word.md): A secret word only your family knows beats a voice clone. Use it whenever an "emergency" call asks for money.
- [Have the family tech talk, and keep having it](../../practices/people-and-culture/ppl-family-tech-talk.md): Agree as a family on devices, apps, sharing and what to do when something online feels wrong, with the door always open.
- [Learn the scam reflex, pause and verify](../../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-pause-and-verify.md): Almost every scam pushes urgency, secrecy or an unusual payment. When you feel that pressure, stop and check through a channel you already trust.
- [Make reporting a phish easy, quick and blame-free](../../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-report-phishing.md): Give people one simple way to report suspicious messages, thank them every time, and act on what they send.
- [Never change payment details on the strength of an email](../../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-payment-change-verification.md): Any request to change bank details, pay a new invoice urgently or buy gift cards gets verified by calling a known number, every time.

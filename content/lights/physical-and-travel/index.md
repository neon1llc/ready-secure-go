Attackers don't care which department owns a problem. A propped-open door, an unlocked network closet or a laptop left in a cafe can undo every digital control. Physical security is also where a lot of real-world harm starts, so I always walk the space.

## Ask yourself

- How many keys or codes to your space exist, and who has them?
- Could a stranger carrying a box follow someone inside?
- What would you lose if your bag were stolen on a trip?

## Practices

<!-- cards:start -->
### 1 · Spark

- [Use public Wi-Fi without worrying about it](../../practices/networks-and-wifi/net-public-wifi.md): Modern encryption makes most public Wi-Fi fine for everyday browsing. Know the few real risks, and when to use your phone's hotspot.

### 2 · Glow

- [Control who can walk into your space](../../practices/physical-and-travel/phys-office-access.md): Know who has keys, badges and codes; log visitors; lock up network gear and paper records; and treat the front door as part of security.
- [Secure your cameras and smart devices](../../practices/devices-and-updates/dev-iot-and-cameras.md): Change default passwords, update firmware, turn off what you don't use, and keep cameras and smart gadgets on their own network.
- [Travel light, travel locked](../../practices/physical-and-travel/phys-travel-kit.md): Before a trip, back up, update and lock down your devices, take only what you need, and plan for loss.

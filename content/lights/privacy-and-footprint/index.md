Privacy is security's quieter twin. Every detail out there about you, from your address to your pet's name to your kids' school, is raw material for scams, account takeovers and worse. You can't erase everything, but you can shrink your footprint and make leaked data useless.

## Ask yourself

- Search your own name and city. What would a stranger learn in two minutes?
- Is your credit frozen? Your kids'?
- Which apps know where you are all the time?

## Practices

<!-- cards:start -->
### 1 · Spark

- [Find out what's already leaked about you](../../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-breach-check.md): Check your email addresses against known breaches, then change any reused passwords that showed up.
- [Freeze your credit (and your kids' credit)](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-credit-freeze.md): A free credit freeze at all three bureaus stops anyone, including you, from opening new credit in your name until you lift it.

### 2 · Glow

- [Do a privacy checkup on your big accounts and phone](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-account-privacy-review.md): Walk through the privacy settings on your phone, Google, Apple, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and similar, and turn off what you didn't mean to share.
- [Get yourself off data broker sites](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-data-broker-opt-out.md): People-search sites sell your address, phone, relatives and age to anyone. Opt out of the biggest ones, or use a service to do it for you.
- [Share files on purpose, not "anyone with the link"](../../practices/data-and-backups/data-share-safely.md): Check who can see your shared files and folders, tighten links, and set expirations for sensitive ones.
- [Understand what's on the dark web about you, and what to do about it](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-dark-web-reality.md): The "dark web" is mostly marketplaces trading stolen logins, card numbers and personal data. Monitoring tells you what leaked. Action is what protects you.

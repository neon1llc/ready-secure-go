## In one sentence

A **SOC 2 report** is an independent CPA firm's opinion on whether your controls for protecting customer data are designed well and, for a Type II, actually working over a period of time.

## What that means in practice

- It's an **attestation report**, not a certification. Nobody "passes SOC 2". An auditor issues an opinion, which can be clean ("unqualified") or note problems ("qualified", plus listed exceptions).
- It's measured against the AICPA's **Trust Services Criteria**. You choose which categories apply. **Security** is always included.
- **You** decide the controls. SOC 2 says *what* must be achieved ("access is restricted to authorized users"), not *how*. A 10-person startup and a bank can both have SOC 2 reports with very different controls.
- The report is usually shared with customers under NDA.

## What it isn't

- **Not a checklist.** There's no list of 100 things you must do. That flexibility is great, and it's also why readiness work matters: you have to design controls that fit.
- **Not ISO 27001.** ISO 27001 is an international *certification* of your information security management system. There's lots of overlap, and many companies eventually do both.
- **Not SOC 1.** SOC 1 covers controls relevant to customers' *financial reporting* (think payroll processors). SOC 2 covers security and related trust categories.
- **Not SOC 3.** SOC 3 is a short, public summary of a SOC 2, for your website.
- **Not a guarantee** you won't be breached. It shows you run a disciplined program.

> [!question] Ask yourself
> - Which customers are asking, and what do they actually need? Sometimes a completed security questionnaire or a Type I is enough to start.
> - Could you show an outsider evidence that your controls work, or would you be describing intentions?

Auditors will read your policies, then check that you actually do what they say. The worst policy is a 40-page template nobody follows, because every gap between the words and reality becomes an exception in your report.

## The rule

**Write down what you really do (or will really do), as briefly as possible, and make sure someone owns each policy.**

## A practical starter set

| Policy | Covers |
|---|---|
| Information security policy | The umbrella: who's responsible, how policies are approved and reviewed |
| Acceptable use | What people can and can't do with company devices, accounts and data |
| Access control | How access is requested, approved, reviewed and removed; MFA; admin accounts |
| Asset management | Inventory, device standards, encryption, disposal |
| Change management | How code and infrastructure changes are reviewed, tested and approved |
| Incident response | Roles, severity levels, steps, communication, lessons learned |
| Business continuity and disaster recovery | Backups, recovery targets, testing |
| Vendor management | How vendors are assessed, approved and reviewed |
| Risk assessment | How often, by whom, and what happens with the results |
| Data classification and retention | Kinds of data, how each is handled, how long it's kept |
| Security awareness | Training at hire and on a schedule |
| HR security | Background checks, confidentiality agreements, onboarding and offboarding |

Smaller companies often combine these into a handful of documents. That's fine.

## Keeping them honest

- **One owner per policy**, reviewed at least yearly, with the review date recorded.
- **People acknowledge them** at hire and yearly (a signature or a checkbox in your HR or compliance tool is evidence).
- **When reality changes, change the policy.** Don't let them drift.

## Templates

Open, adaptable starting points exist. For example, JupiterOne's security policy templates (CC BY-SA 4.0) map to SOC 2, HIPAA and NIST. Adapt them heavily. A template that says you do things you don't is worse than no template.

title: "Kit: staying safe online, for parents and teachers"
description: "A 60 to 75 minute session for parents and teachers on scams, accounts, privacy, and kids online."
tags: [teach, kit]
---

**Audience:** parents, teachers, school staff. **Group size:** 5 to 40. **Length:** 60 to 75 minutes. **You'll need:** a projector, a phone for live demos, and printed [home checklists](../printables/home-spark-checklist.md).

## Objectives

Participants can:

1. Spot the four pressures behind almost every scam, and verify before acting.
2. Explain why unique passwords and two-step login stop most account takeovers, and set one up.
3. Name two ways their family's information leaks online, and one way to shrink it.
4. Say what to do, and not do, if a child is threatened or blackmailed online.

## Hook (5 min)

Ask: *"Raise your hand if you've gotten a text about a package you didn't order."* Almost every hand goes up. *"Keep it up if you've gotten a call or email claiming to be your bank, the IRS, or Amazon."* Then: *"Every one of those is the same trick. Tonight you'll learn to spot it."*

## Explanation (20 min)

1. **How scams work** (7 min): urgency, secrecy, authority or emotion, and strange payment ([Learn the scam reflex, pause and verify](../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-pause-and-verify.md)). Show three real examples and have the room spot the pressures.
2. **Why accounts get taken over** (7 min): breaches, credential stuffing, and email as the master key. Then password managers and two-step login ([Let a password manager remember for you](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-password-manager.md), [Turn on two-step login, starting with your email](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-two-step-login.md)).
3. **Kids online** (6 min): the biggest risks are people, not hackers: sextortion, scams in games, and oversharing. The best protection is a kid who'll tell you ([Have the family tech talk, and keep having it](../practices/people-and-culture/ppl-family-tech-talk.md)).

## Activities (30 min)

- **Breach check, live** (10 min): with a volunteer's permission, check their email on Have I Been Pwned on screen and talk through what leaked ([Find out what's already leaked about you](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-breach-check.md)). Then everyone searches their own name and town on their phone ([Get yourself off data broker sites](../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-data-broker-opt-out.md)).
- **Two-step login, live** (10 min): set it up on a volunteer's email or a demo account, including saving backup codes. Point out where a thief would be stopped.
- **Pause and verify** (10 min): in pairs, one reads a scam scenario card ("Grandma, it's me, I'm in trouble and need bail money...") and the other practices pause-and-verify out loud. Swap. Introduce the family code word ([Agree on a family code word for emergencies](../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-family-code-word.md)).

## Misconceptions to address

- *"I'm not important enough to be hacked."* Most attacks are automated and aimed at everyone.
- *"Text-message codes are perfect."* App codes and passkeys are better.
- *"Parental controls handle it."* They help, but talking protects more.
- *"If my kid is being blackmailed, paying will make it stop."* It almost never does. Don't pay, save evidence, report ([Playbook: my child is in trouble online](../playbooks/kid-in-trouble-online.md)).
- *"I need a VPN to be safe."* Usually not for everyday browsing ([Use public Wi-Fi without worrying about it](../practices/networks-and-wifi/net-public-wifi.md)).

## Check for understanding (5 min)

1. A text says your bank account is locked and gives a link. What do you do?
2. Which account do you protect first, and why?
3. Your child says someone online is threatening to share a photo unless they pay. What are your first three steps?

Then hand out the [home checklist](../printables/home-spark-checklist.md). Everyone does one item before leaving or picks one for tonight.

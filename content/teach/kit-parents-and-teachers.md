---
title: "Kit: staying safe online, for parents and teachers"
description: "A 60 to 75 minute session for parents and teachers on scams, accounts, privacy and kids online."
tags: [teach, kit]
---

**Audience:** parents, teachers, school staff. **Group size:** 5 to 40. **Length:** 60 to 75 minutes. **You'll need:** a projector, a phone for live demos, and printed [home checklists](../printables/home-spark-checklist.md).

## Objectives

By the end, participants can:

1. Spot the four pressures behind almost every scam, and verify before acting.
2. Explain why unique passwords and two-step login stop most account takeovers, and set one up.
3. Name at least two ways their family's information leaks online, and one way to shrink it.
4. Describe what to do, and what *not* to do, if a child is threatened or blackmailed online.

## Hook (5 min)

Ask: *"Raise your hand if you've gotten a text about a package you didn't order."* Almost every hand goes up. *"Keep it up if you've gotten a call or email claiming to be your bank, the IRS or Amazon."* Then: *"Every one of those is the same trick wearing different clothes. Tonight you'll learn to see the trick."*

## Explanation (20 min)

1. **How scams work** (7 min): the four pressures, which are urgency, secrecy, authority or emotion, and strange payment ([Learn the scam reflex, pause and verify](../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-pause-and-verify.md)). Show three real examples and have the room spot the pressures.
2. **Why accounts get taken over** (7 min): breaches, credential stuffing, and why your email is the master key. Password managers and two-step login ([Let a password manager remember for you](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-password-manager.md), [Turn on two-step login, starting with your email](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-two-step-login.md)).
3. **Kids online** (6 min): the biggest risks aren't hackers, they're people. Sextortion, scams in games, oversharing. The most protective thing is a kid who'll tell you ([Have the family tech talk, and keep having it](../practices/people-and-culture/ppl-family-tech-talk.md)).

## Interactive (10 min)

- **Breach check, live:** with a volunteer's permission, check their email on Have I Been Pwned on screen ([Find out what's already leaked about you](../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-breach-check.md)). Talk through what leaked and what it means.
- **Search yourself:** everyone searches their own name and town on their phone. What turns up? ([Get yourself off data broker sites](../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-data-broker-opt-out.md))

## Worked example (10 min)

Set up two-step login on a volunteer's email (or your own demo account) on the projector, step by step, including saving backup codes. Point out the moment a thief would be stopped.

## Practice (10 min)

Pairs: one reads a scam scenario card ("Grandma, it's me, I'm in trouble and need bail money..."), the other practices the pause-and-verify response out loud. Swap. Introduce the family code word ([Agree on a family code word for emergencies](../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-family-code-word.md)).

## Misconceptions to address

- *"I'm not important enough to be hacked."* Most attacks are automated and aimed at everyone.
- *"Text-message codes are perfect."* They're good, but app codes and passkeys are better.
- *"Parental controls handle it."* Controls help, but conversation protects.
- *"If my kid is being blackmailed, paying will make it stop."* It almost never does. Don't pay, save evidence, report ([Playbook: my child is in trouble online](../playbooks/kid-in-trouble-online.md)).
- *"I need a VPN to be safe."* Usually not for everyday browsing ([Use public Wi-Fi without worrying about it](../practices/networks-and-wifi/net-public-wifi.md)).

## Check for understanding (5 min)

Ask the room:

1. A text says your bank account is locked and gives a link. What do you do?
2. What's the one account you should protect first, and why?
3. Your child says someone online is threatening to share a photo unless they pay. What are your first three steps?

## Leave with one action

Hand out the [home checklist](../printables/home-spark-checklist.md). Everyone does one item before leaving, or promises a specific one tonight.

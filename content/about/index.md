## Why this exists

neon1 started at a grade school.

I was working as an after-school aide and helped budget and set up a new STEM edtech system for the school. Teachers and parents kept asking me the same thing: *how do I keep my kids, my classroom, and my own accounts safe?* So I invited them to free security debriefings, with no acronym-filled slides: what's out there, why it works on people, and what to do about it.

That became neon1. Since then I've run sessions for teachers, parents, engineers, executives, and cross-team groups, including seminars in Manhattan for groups of 30 or more. I've also done SOC 2 and other security readiness work for companies.

This collection is those sessions written down.

## What I believe about security

- **Security is for everyone.** A grandparent who spots a scam call is doing security. So is a teacher who turns on two-step login.
- **Understanding beats memorizing.** If you know *why* something works, you'll recognize the next trick.
- **Start small, then keep going.** A few habits block much of the real-world harm. You just need to not be the easiest target.
- **Look at the whole picture.** With every client I look at accounts, devices, networks, cameras, the front door, the people, and the vendors. Attackers don't care which department owns a problem.
- **No fear selling.** Honest risk, honest odds, honest fixes.

## The names

**Ready, Secure, Go** borrows the start of a race: get ready, lock down what matters, then get on with your day.

**neon1** comes from two characters. Neo, "the One" in *The Matrix*, is an ordinary person who chooses to help other people see clearly. Andrew, the robot in *Bicentennial Man*, spends two hundred years being of service and never stops being curious. Neo also means new, and neon is hard to miss. The 1 is whoever I'm working with.

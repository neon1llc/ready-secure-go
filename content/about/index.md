## Why this exists

neon1 started at a grade school.

I was working as an after-school aide and helped budget and set up a new STEM edtech system for the school. Teachers and parents kept asking me the same thing: *how do I keep my kids, my classroom, and my own accounts safe?* So I invited them to free security debriefings. No slides full of acronyms. Just what's out there, why it works on people, and what to do about it.

Watching people leave a little more confident than they came in gave me the confidence to offer the same help more widely. That became neon1. Since then I've run sessions for teachers, parents, engineers, executives, and cross-team groups, including seminars in Manhattan. I've also done SOC 2 and other security readiness work for companies.

This collection is those sessions written down and made free for anyone.

## What I believe about security

- **Security is for everyone.** A grandparent who spots a scam call is doing security. So is a teacher who turns on two-step login.
- **Understanding beats memorizing.** If you know *why* something works, you'll recognize the next trick.
- **Start small, then keep going.** Five habits stop most real-world harm. You just need to not be the easiest target.
- **Look at the whole picture.** With every client I look at accounts, devices, networks, cameras, the front door, the people, and the vendors. Attackers don't care which department owns a problem.
- **No fear selling.** Honest risk, honest odds, honest fixes.

## The names

**Ready, Secure, Go** borrows the start of a race. Get ready, lock down what matters, then get on with your day.

**neon1**, the company behind it, comes from two characters. Neo, "the One" in *The Matrix*, is an ordinary person who chooses to help other people see clearly. Andrew, the robot in *Bicentennial Man*, spends two hundred years being of service and never stops being curious.

Neo also means new, and neon is hard to miss. The 1 is whoever I'm working with. That's you.

*1 is glad to be of service.*

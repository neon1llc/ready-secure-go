title: Seniors & Caregivers
description: "Older adults and the people who help them: staying connected while staying out of scammers' reach."
---

Older adults are targeted more often and lose more per scam, according to the FBI's yearly elder fraud reports. That's not because they're less capable. It's because scammers go where the savings are, and they're skilled at exploiting kindness, trust and isolation. Caregivers can help a lot, as long as it's done *with* the person, not to them.

## Ask yourself

- Has anyone ever called claiming to be from Medicare, the IRS, Amazon, the bank, or "tech support"? What happened?
- If a grandchild called crying and asked for bail money, what would happen next?
- Who would they call first if something felt wrong? Do they know they can call you without embarrassment?

**If money was already lost:** call the bank right away, then the **National Elder Fraud Hotline, 1-833-FRAUD-11 (1-833-372-8311)**, run by the U.S. Department of Justice. Also see the [scam playbook](../../playbooks/scam-victim.md).

## Everything for this space, by level

<!-- cards:start -->
### 1 · Spark

- [Agree on a family code word for emergencies](../../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-family-code-word.md): A secret word only your family knows beats a voice clone. Use it whenever an "emergency" call asks for money.
- [Back up what you can't lose, the 3-2-1 way](../../practices/data-and-backups/data-3-2-1-backup.md): Three copies, on two kinds of storage, with one kept somewhere else, and at least one that ransomware can't reach.
- [Find out what's already leaked about you](../../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-breach-check.md): Check your email addresses against known breaches, then change any reused passwords that showed up.
- [Freeze your credit (and your kids' credit)](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-credit-freeze.md): A free credit freeze at all three bureaus stops anyone, including you, from opening new credit in your name until you lift it.
- [Have the family tech talk, and keep having it](../../practices/people-and-culture/ppl-family-tech-talk.md): Agree as a family on devices, apps, sharing and what to do when something online feels wrong, with the door always open.
- [Learn the scam reflex, pause and verify](../../practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-pause-and-verify.md): Almost every scam pushes urgency, secrecy or an unusual payment. When you feel that pressure, stop and check through a channel you already trust.
- [Let a password manager remember for you](../../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-password-manager.md): One strong password you memorize, and a different random one for every account, kept by an app that fills them in for you.
- [Lock your screens and encrypt your devices](../../practices/devices-and-updates/dev-screen-lock-and-encryption.md): A passcode plus full-disk encryption turns a lost or stolen device from a data breach into just a lost device.
- [Make sure you can get back into your own accounts](../../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-recovery-check.md): Update recovery emails and phone numbers, save backup codes, and remove old ones an ex, a former employee or a stranger could use.
- [Set up find-my-device and remote wipe before you need it](../../practices/devices-and-updates/dev-find-and-wipe.md): Turn on location and remote erase now, so a lost phone or laptop can be found, locked or wiped from any browser.
- [Set up your router like you mean it](../../practices/networks-and-wifi/net-router-basics.md): New admin password, strong Wi-Fi encryption, current firmware, and the risky convenience features turned off.
- [Turn on automatic updates, everywhere](../../practices/devices-and-updates/dev-auto-updates.md): Let phones, computers, browsers, apps and routers fix their own security holes without waiting for you.
- [Turn on two-step login, starting with your email](../../practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-two-step-login.md): Add a second proof, like an app code, a security key or a passkey, so a stolen password alone isn't enough.
- [Use public Wi-Fi without worrying about it](../../practices/networks-and-wifi/net-public-wifi.md): Modern encryption makes most public Wi-Fi fine for everyday browsing. Know the few real risks, and when to use your phone's hotspot.
- [Write your "if something goes wrong" sheet, on paper](../../practices/respond-and-recover/resp-incident-contact-sheet.md): One page with who to call, how to reach them without your computer, and the first three things to do.

### 2 · Glow

- [Do a privacy checkup on your big accounts and phone](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-account-privacy-review.md): Walk through the privacy settings on your phone, Google, Apple, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and similar, and turn off what you didn't mean to share.
- [Get yourself off data broker sites](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-data-broker-opt-out.md): People-search sites sell your address, phone, relatives and age to anyone. Opt out of the biggest ones, or use a service to do it for you.
- [Retire devices that can't get updates, and wipe them first](../../practices/devices-and-updates/dev-retire-old-devices.md): Replace hardware that no longer gets security updates, and properly erase anything you sell, donate or recycle.
- [Secure your cameras and smart devices](../../practices/devices-and-updates/dev-iot-and-cameras.md): Change default passwords, update firmware, turn off what you don't use, and keep cameras and smart gadgets on their own network.
- [Understand what's on the dark web about you, and what to do about it](../../practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-dark-web-reality.md): The "dark web" is mostly marketplaces trading stolen logins, card numbers and personal data. Monitoring tells you what leaked. Action is what protects you.

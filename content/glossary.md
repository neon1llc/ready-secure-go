---
title: Glossary
description: "Security words in plain language."
---

**Attack surface**: everything an attacker could try to get in through: accounts, devices, internet-facing services, people, doors.

**Backup (3-2-1)**: three copies of your data, on two kinds of storage, one kept elsewhere. See [Back up what you can't lose, the 3-2-1 way](practices/data-and-backups/data-3-2-1-backup.md).

**BEC (business email compromise)**: scams that use real or look-alike business email to trick people into sending money or data. See [Never change payment details on the strength of an email](practices/scams-and-social-engineering/scam-payment-change-verification.md).

**Credential stuffing**: trying leaked username and password pairs on many other sites, automatically.

**Credit freeze**: a free block on your credit report so nobody can open new credit in your name. See [Freeze your credit (and your kids' credit)](practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-credit-freeze.md).

**Dark web**: websites reachable only with special software like Tor. The criminal part trades stolen data. See [Understand what's on the dark web about you, and what to do about it](practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-dark-web-reality.md).

**Data broker**: a company that collects and sells personal information. See [Get yourself off data broker sites](practices/privacy-and-footprint/priv-data-broker-opt-out.md).

**Encryption**: scrambling data so only someone with the key can read it. "Full-disk encryption" protects a whole device if it's lost.

**Firmware**: the built-in software that runs a device like a router or camera. It needs updates too.

**IoT (Internet of Things)**: everyday devices connected to the internet: cameras, doorbells, thermostats, smart TVs.

**Least privilege**: giving each person and system only the access they need, nothing more.

**Malware**: any software designed to harm: viruses, spyware, ransomware.

**MFA (multi-factor authentication) or two-step login**: proving who you are with two different kinds of evidence, like a password plus a phone or key. See [Turn on two-step login, starting with your email](practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-two-step-login.md).

**MITRE ATT&CK**: a public catalog of real attacker techniques, each with an ID like T1566 (Phishing).

**Passkey**: a login that uses a key stored on your device instead of a password. It's phishing-resistant. See [Switch important accounts to passkeys](practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-passkeys.md).

**Patch or update**: a fix from the maker, often closing a security hole.

**Phishing**: messages that trick you into clicking, sharing or paying. Variants: **smishing** (texts), **vishing** (voice calls), **spear phishing** (targeted).

**Ransomware**: malware that locks your files and demands payment, often after stealing a copy.

**Sextortion**: blackmail using real or fake intimate images, often aimed at teenagers. See [Playbook: my child is in trouble online](playbooks/kid-in-trouble-online.md).

**SIM swap**: tricking a phone carrier into moving your number to a criminal's SIM, so they get your text codes.

**SOC 2**: an independent audit report on how a company protects customer data. See [SOC 2 track](tracks/soc2/index.md).

**Social engineering**: manipulating people, rather than technology, to get access or information.

**Tabletop exercise**: a practice run of an incident, talked through around a table. See [Practice a bad day with a tabletop exercise](practices/respond-and-recover/resp-tabletop-exercise.md).

**Tailgating**: following someone through a secured door without using your own badge or key.

**VPN**: an encrypted tunnel for your internet traffic to a provider you trust. Useful in specific situations, not magic. See [Use public Wi-Fi without worrying about it](practices/networks-and-wifi/net-public-wifi.md).

**Zero trust**: a design approach where no device or user is trusted automatically just for being "inside" the network. Everything is verified.
